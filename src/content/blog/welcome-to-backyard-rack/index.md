Welcome to **The Backyard Rack**!

If you've followed my Kubernetes, Proxmox, OpenStack, and general infrastructure adventures over on Medium, you might have noticed something different. I've packed up my YAML, grabbed my Helm charts, and moved out. I'm no longer publishing on Medium. Not because I've stopped writing; quite the opposite. It's because Medium has become a big hot festering pile of shit.

There. Said it.

## Medium, come on mate!

Once upon a time, Medium was a decent place to publish technical articles. You wrote something useful, hit publish, and people who were interested in the subject could actually find it. Occasionally, someone would read the whole thing without being asked to sign up or become a bloody paid member.

What a concept, righto? But somewhere along the way, the experience changed. The platform increasingly feels like it's optimised for everything *except* connecting writers with readers.

**And then there's the AI circus**. The internet is drowning in mass-produced, AI-generated content. Endless articles promising to **revolutionise**, **supercharge**, and **unlock the power of** whatever technology was trending five minutes ago. (add quoatations wherever you see fit)

You know the ones:

*"10 Kubernetes Secrets That Will Change Your Life!"* or *"Kubernetes is Dead -- Here is why!"*.

Written by *something* that has apparently never seen a Kubernetes cluster, let alone watched one catch fire at 2 a.m. Meanwhile, those of us actually building things, breaking things, fixing things, and documenting what happened are left competing for attention in an increasingly noisy feed. I'm not claiming AI is the root of all evil. It's a useful tool, and I like useful tools. But there's a difference between using AI to help you work and flooding the internet with confident-sounding technical sludge. And when discovery and traffic increasingly depend on opaque algorithms, it's hard to justify putting hours into detailed technical writing on a platform where you have so little control over who sees it.

Hence, welcome to **The Backyard Rack**!

## And No, I'm Not Moving to Substack Either

Every time someone complains about Medium, there's inevitably a well-meaning suggestion: *"Have you considered Substack?"*

**Yes. For about three seconds.**

Look, I'm sure Substack is lovely if your lifelong ambition is to become a writer (hahahahaha). But I don't want to. **I want to run a bloody blog.**

I write technical articles about Kubernetes, Proxmox, OpenStack, and other infrastructure nonsense. I want people to find them when they're Googling an obscure error at 2 a.m., not receive a surprise essay in their inbox while they're trying to find a parcel delivery notification. I don't need subscribers, email campaigns, open rates, conversion funnels, or a weekly obligation to produce *content*. I'm not launching a media company. I'm documenting stuff I've built, broken, and occasionally managed to fix.

And I certainly don't want to replace one publishing platform's algorithmic nonsense with another platform's obsession with turning every writer into a newsletter *entrepreneur*.

Just a website (with RSS). Like we used to have. **Radical concept, apparently.**

## So I Built My Own Little Corner of the Internet

Welcome to **The Backyard Rack**.

The name is exactly what it sounds like: a nod to the kind of infrastructure experiments that start with a perfectly reasonable idea and the unjustified confidence of a junior developer:

*"Surely this won't be too difficult."*, Famous last words, my young Padawan!

This is now the home for my technical articles, experiments, tutorials, and occasional infrastructure-induced rage.

Expect plenty of:

- **Kubernetes**, because apparently I enjoy making simple things distributed (it's a microservice architecture you wouldn't understand).
- **Proxmox and virtualisation**, there's always room for another VM (not with these RAM prices mate!).
- **OpenStack and cloud infrastructure**, for when Kubernetes alone isn't complicated enough.
- **Automation, networking, and self-hosting**, no amount of CVEs can deter us from living on the edge.
- **Real-world troubleshooting**, including the bits that didn't work the first time (it's called wisdom kiddo)

The focus hasn't changed. I just break things and document them so you don't need to break your things. (swearing is optional)

## What's Different Here?

For starters, **this is my site**.

No Medium membership walls (never had any anyways) between you and an article. No platform deciding whether a technical deep dive deserves to appear in someone's feed. No need to optimise every headline for an algorithm having a midlife crisis. Just articles, code, diagrams, and occasional bad jokes and puns.

The site is built with **Astro**, and I've gradually been moving my existing articles across so they can continue to be useful without depending on Medium.

I'm also giving the content a bit of a tidy-up or a rewrite as I go. Think of it as moving house, except instead of discovering boxes full of old cables, I'm rediscovering YAML manifests and wondering "what the heck was I thinking that day dammit!"

## To Everyone Who Followed Me on Medium

**Thanks, seriously.**

If you found your way over from Medium, welcome back. If you've just stumbled across this place while searching for an obscure Kubernetes error message, welcome aboard.

Pull up a chair. The coffee's probably gone cold, and something in the cluster is almost certainly restarting.

## Same Content. Less Corporate Bullshit.

I'm not trying to build a content empire.

I'm not interested in publishing five AI-generated articles a day about why the "latest CNCF project will *change everything*". Oh, CNCF Ambassadors and MVPs, I'll poke that particular wound another day. You really are the worst sometimes, you know?

I just want somewhere to write about interesting technical problems, explain how I approached them, and share the occasional spectacular failure.

If that helps someone build something, learn something, or avoid a particularly nasty rabbit hole, then the effort was worth it.

So that's the story. Medium was an interesting side-quest. But I'm packing my shit and going home.

Welcome to **The Backyard Rack**.

Now, if you'll excuse me, I've got a cluster that's been running suspiciously quietly for days...

**— Kyr**
